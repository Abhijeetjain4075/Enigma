# Competitive Strategy Patterns — 2026-09-30

This is a descriptive strategy map, not a ranking.

## Hubject / intercharge

Observed strategy:

- aggregate a global roaming ecosystem
- sell one integration to CPOs/eMSPs
- reduce bilateral agreement complexity
- provide authorization and settlement
- expand Plug & Charge and certification/trust services
- operate financial services alongside interoperability

Current public scale claims: 1.1M+ charge points, 3,500+ B2B partners, 75+ countries.

Source:
https://www.hubject.com/intercharge-overview

**Implication for Enigma:** “one connection to many networks” is already a major business model. Enigma needs a layer above or orthogonal to the hub, not another hub clone.

## GIREVE

Observed strategy:

- B2B marketplace/transaction/data platform
- roaming and clearing
- partner agreement management
- Plug & Charge PKI/pools
- testing/certification
- parking and MaaS adjacency
- expansion through partner connectivity

Current public figures include 680,000 connected charging points and 13,000 agreements.

Sources:
- https://www.gireve.com/
- https://www.gireve.com/plug-and-charge-services/

**Implication:** GIREVE already demonstrates that roaming can expand toward adjacent mobility and trust services.

## e-clearing.net

## Strategic synthesis

The strongest incumbent patterns are:

1. Network aggregation.
2. Protocol translation.
3. Authorization.
4. CDR exchange.
5. Settlement/reconciliation.
6. Consumer distribution.
7. OEM/fleet distribution.
8. Plug & Charge trust.
9. CPMS/CSMS control.
10. Energy optimization.
11. Data quality/enrichment.
12. Adjacent parking/MaaS.

Enigma must not claim novelty merely by combining these words.

The only credible unexplored area is a **measurable residual workflow** that existing combinations fail to provide economically or technically.
